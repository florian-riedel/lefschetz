We deduce a topological and homotopical Lefschetz fixed point 
via sheaves and local systems. Throughout this entire section, we fix a base 
topological space $M$ which locally compact and Hausdorff.

\begin{notation}
  Let \mdef{$\Top$} denote the category of \tdef{locally compact Hausdorff spaces}
  with continuous maps between them.
  Moreover, we write $\mdef{\pTop{\base}}, \mdef{\psTop{\base}}\subseteq \Top_{/\base}$ 
  for the full subcategory spanned by the \tdef{proper} maps and
  \tdef{proper shape submersions} $p\colon X\to \base$ respectively.
\end{notation}


\begin{defn}\label{defn: dynamical system}
  A \tdef{dynamical system} over $\base$ is a tuple $(X,p,f)$ where
  $(p\colon X\to \base)\in \pTop{\base}$ and $f\colon X\to X$ is a self map over $\base$. 
  We say that $(X,p,f)$ is \tdef{shapely} if additionally
  $(p\colon X\to \base)\in \psTop{\base}$. 
\end{defn}

We think of $f$ as the evolution of the dynamical system and $\base$ as a space of parameters.
For example, if $\base = \RR$ and $p_1\colon\RR\times\tset\to\RR$ is the projection onto
the first coordinate, then a dynamical system $(\RR\times \tset ,p_1, f)$
as above is uniquely determined by the ``flow'' map 
\[\phi\colon\RR\times\tset\too\tset,\quad (t,x)\mapsto p_2f(t,x),\]
where $p_2\colon\RR\times\tset\to\tset$ denotes the projection onto the second coordinate. 
(Note however that the map $\phi$ arising in this way will generally not be compatible 
with the group structure on $\RR$.)

  \[ \cD \colon \sSpan{\cC}_{\cP,\cI}\too \mathscr{V}\]

  for the associated cohomology theory 
  \[ \cH_{\cD}(-) \colon \Omega \sSpan{\cC}_{\cP,\cI}\too \Omega\mathscr{V}\,.\]

    where $f\in \cP$ and $g\in \cI$, we learn that the trace of the induced
    map on cohomology $\cH_{\cD}(\Lambda)\colon \cH_{\cD}(X)\to \cH_{\cD}(X)$ naturally factors

\subsection{Topological Lefschetz}\label{subsec: topological lefschetz}

The key insight is that, given a dynamical system $(X,p,f)$, working with the category
$\pSp{X}$ allows us to express the cohomology of the fixed points of $f$ as a categorified
trace, which is the content of the following lemma:

\begin{lem}\label{lem: top trace identification}
    Let $(X,p,f)$ be a dynamical system over $\base$ with
    fixed points $ X^f\subseteq X$. Then the $\infty$-category $\pSp{X}\in \pPrl{\base}$
    is dualizable and have an equivalence:
    \[ \tr{f_!\colon \pSp{X}\to \pSp{X}}{\pPrl{\base}}\simeq \cptSec{X^f}[\base]\in \pSp{\base}\,.\]
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  Since the functor $\pSp{(-)}$ is symmetric monoidal and $X\in \Span{\pTop{\base}}$ is
  dualizable by \cref{lem: duality datum}, it follows that $\pSp{X}$
  is dualizable.
  Moreover, by the naturality statement of \cref{thm: trace functor}, the symmetric monoidal 
  $(\infty,2)$-functor $\pSpf{(-)}$ induces a symmetric monoidal functor 
  \[ \pSpf{(-)}\colon \Endtrl(\sSpan{\pTop{\base}}_{\cP,\cI}) 
  \too \Endtrl(\pPrlf{\base})\,,\]
  which is compatible with trace. Thus, we learn that 
 the trace of the functor $f_!\colon \pSp{X}\to \pSp{X}$
 is computed as the image of the trace of the span 
 $(X\xleftarrow{\id} X \xrightarrow{f}X)$, which by \cref{lem: trace in Span}
 is given by the fixed points $\base \leftarrow X^f \to \base$,
 which was the claim. 
\end{proof}

If the dynamical system is additionally assumed to be \emph{shapely} in the sense
of \cref{defn: dynamical system}, we can define generalized fixed point
indices as well as a Lefschetz number. 

\begin{lem}
  For any proper shape submersion $p\colon X\to \base$, the associated cohomology sheaf
  $\cptSec{X}[\base] \in \pSp{\base}$ is dualizable.
\end{lem}

In particular, the following is well defined:

\begin{defn}\label{defn: Lefschetz class}
 Let $(X,p,f)$ be a shapely dynamical system over $\base$. We define the associated
 \tdef{Lefschetz class} to be the trace:
 \[ \mdef{L(f)}\coloneq \tr{\pb{f} \colon \cptSec{X}[\base] 
 \to \cptSec{X}[\base]}{\pSp{\base}}\,.\]
\end{defn}

Moreover, we can refine this class to a fixed point index via the following construction.

\begin{cons}\label{cons: Lefschetz index}
    Let $(X,p,f)$ be a shapely dynamical system over $\base$. 
    Consider the following diagram in $\pPrlf{\base}$:
   \[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\pSp{X}} & {\pSp{\base}} \\
	{\pSp{X}} & {\pSp{\base}}
	\arrow["{p_!}", from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{f_!}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["\id", from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow[Rightarrow, from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{p_!}"', from=2-1, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd} \]
where the 2-cell is induced by the equation
$pf=p$. Since $p$ is a proper shape submersion,
the functor $p_!$ admits an internal right adjoint by REF/CITE,
and hence this defines a morphism $(\pSp{X},f_!)\to (\pSp{\base},\id)$ in $\Endtrl(\pPrlf{\base})$. Applying the symmetric
monoidal functor 
\[ \TrcL\colon \Endtrl(\pPrlf{\base}) \to \pSp{\base}\]
of \cref{thm: trace functor}
and using the identification of \cref{lem: top trace identification} we obtain
a map:
\[ \mdef{\lambda^f}\colon \cptSec{X^f}[\base]
\to \SS_{\base} \in \pSp{\base}\]
which we call the \tdef{Lefschetz index} of $f$. 
\end{cons}

The proof of the main result of this section is now straightforward.

\begin{thm}\label{thm: Top Lefschetz}
Let $(X,p,f)$ be a shapely dynamical system over $\base$,
$\lambda^f\colon \cptSec{X^f}[\base] \to \SS_{\base}$ be
the Lefschetz index of \cref{cons: Lefschetz index} and write 
$q\colon X^f\to \base$ for the terminal map from the fixed points of $f$.
The composite 
\[ \SS_{\base} \xrightarrow{\pb{q}} \cptSec{X^f}[\base]
\xrightarrow{\lambda^f} \SS_{\base}\,\]
is given by the Lefschetz class $L(f)$ of \cref{defn: Lefschetz class}. 
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
  Since $p\colon X\to \base$ is a proper shape submersion by assumption, the two functors 
  $p^*$ and $p_!$ admit internal right adjoints by \todo{REF/CITE} and hence we may
  consider the commutative diagram in $\Endtrl(\pPrlf{\base})$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq: categorical factorization top}
   \begin{tikzcd}
	{\pSp{\base}} & {\pSp{X}} & {\pSp{\base}} \\
	{\pSp{\base}} & {\pSp{X}} & {\pSp{\base}}
	\arrow["{p^*}", from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["\id"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["{p_!}", from=1-2, to=1-3]
	\arrow["{f_!}", from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow["\id", from=1-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow["\alpha", Rightarrow, from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{p^*}"', from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["\beta", Rightarrow, from=2-2, to=1-3]
	\arrow["{p_!}"', from=2-2, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd} 
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha\colon f_!p^*\to p^*$ is adjoint to the preferred equivalence $f^*p^* = p^*$ and $\beta$ is the preferred
    equivalence $p_!f_!=p_!$. Applying the functor 
    \[ \TrcL\colon \Endtrl(\pPrlf{\base})\to \pSp{\base}\,\]
    and using the identification of \cref{lem: top trace identification} 
    we obtain a commutative diagram in $\pSp{\base}$ of the form:
    \[
\begin{tikzcd}
	{} & {\cptSec{X^f}[\base]} & \\
	{\SS_{\base}} && {\SS_{\base}}
	\arrow["{\lambda^f}", from=1-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["{\TrcL(p^*,\alpha)}", from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{\TrcL(p_!p^*, \beta\alpha)}"', from=2-1, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}
    \]
    The map $\beta\alpha\colon p_!p^*\SS_{\base}\to p_!p^*\SS_{\base}$ is by definition
    the pullback $\pb{f}$ and so by construction of the functor 
    $\TrcL$ we have that
    \[ \TrcL(p_!p^*,\beta\alpha)= \tr{\pb{f}}{\pSp{\base}} = L(f)\,.\]
    It remains to show that $\TrcL(p^*,\alpha)$ is given
    by the pullback $\pb{q}$ along the projection 
    map $q\colon X^{f}\to \base$.
    Indeed, by \cref{lem: internal adjoints} the span 
    $(\base \xleftarrow{p} X \xrightarrow{\id} X)$ admits an internal
    right adjoint give by the flipped span. Thus, it defines a map:
    \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
	\base & X & X \\
	\base && X \\
	\base & X & X 
	\arrow["p"', from=1-2, to=1-1]
	\arrow["\id", from=1-2, to=1-3]
	\arrow["\id"', from=2-1, to=1-1]
	\arrow["\id", from=2-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["f", from=2-3, to=1-3]
	\arrow["\id"', from=2-3, to=3-3]
	\arrow["p"', from=3-2, to=3-1]
	\arrow["\id", from=3-2, to=3-3]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
in $\Endtrl(\sSpan{\pTop{\base}}_{\cP,\cI})$ whose image under the functor 
$\pSpf{(-)}$ is the left hand square of \eqref{eq: categorical factorization top}. Thus, unwinding the definitions and using the naturality of the
functor $\TrcL$, we learn that the map $\TrcL(p^*,\alpha)$
is the image of the 2-morphism: 
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	& {X^f} & \\
	\base & {X^f} & \base \\
	& \base
	\arrow["q"', from=1-2, to=2-1]
	\arrow["q", from=1-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["\id"', from=2-2, to=1-2]
	\arrow["q", from=2-2, to=3-2]
	\arrow[from=3-2, to=2-1]
	\arrow[from=3-2, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
under the functor $\cptSec{-}[\base]$, which is exactly the pullback along 
$q\colon X^f \to \base$. 
\end{proof}

Write $H^*(\base)$ for the sheaf cohomology of our base space $\base$.
As a consequence of \cref{thm: Top Lefschetz}, we immediately deduce the following: 

\begin{cor}\label{cor: Lefschetz finite fixed pts}
  For any shapely dynamical system $(X,p,f)$ over $\base$, the following hold:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $L(f)\neq 0$, then $f$ has a fixed point.
    \item If additionally  $X^f\simeq \base \times S$ for some finite set $S$, then 
      there exist classes $[\lambda^f_s]\in H^0(\base)$ for each $s\in S$ such that:
  \[ L(f)= \sum_{s\in S}[\lambda_s^f]\in H^{0}(\base)\]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{cor}

In particular, for $\base = \pt$ we have $H^0(M)=\ZZ$ and $\cptSec{X}[\base]=C^*(X;\SS)$ 
so we obtain the fixed point formula of \cite{Dold_Puppe1980}.
Moreover, using that the composite functor 
\[ \Sp \xrightarrow{-\otimes \QQ} \Sp_{\QQ} \xrightarrow{\pi_{*}} \mathrm{grVect}_{\QQ}\]
is strong symmetric monoidal, we recover the classical Lefschetz fixed 
point theorem of \cite{Lefschetz1937}.

where $\gamma$ is the preferred equivalence $f^{*}p^*=p^*$ and 
$\delta$ is adjoint to the preferred equivalence $p_\sharp f_{\sharp}= p_{\sharp}$. Applying the functor $\TrcL$, we obtain a commutative diagram:
    \[\begin{tikzcd}
	{} & {\SS_{\base}[\cL^fA]} & \\
	{\SS_{\base}} && {\SS_{\base}}
	\arrow["{\TrcL(p_{\sharp},\delta})", from=1-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["{\rho_f}", from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{\TrcL(p_\sharp p^*, \delta\gamma)}"', from=2-1, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
   Unwinding the definitions the lower horizontal map naturally identifies with the trace
   of the pushforward map. Moreover, we can identify the right hand
   diagonal map with the pushforward along the terminal map 
   $ip\colon \cL^fA\to \pt_\base$ using \cref{lem: internal adjoints}.
\end{proof}

Let us discuss the relation between \cref{thm: Top Lefschetz} and what is 
done in \cref{subsec: topological lefschetz}
explicitly. For this, we need the following notion of relative shape/homotopy type:

\begin{cons}
    Let $\psTop{\base}$ be the full subcategory of 
    $\pTop{\base}$ spanned by those $X\to \base$ which are shape
    submersions.
    For any $X\in \psTop{\base}$ and any (small enough) $U\subseteq \base$, the 
    topological space $\Map_{/\base}(U,X)$ has constant
    shape. Thus, we may define the \tdef{relative shape} 
    of $X$ as the sheaf of spaces $\mdef{\ani{X}[\base]}\in \pSpc{\base}$
    given by the sheafification of the presheaf of spaces
    $\ani{\Map_{\Top_{/\base}}(-,X)} \in \Fun(\mathrm{Open}(\base)^\op,\Spc)$. 
    \todo{Find citations and make sure this is actually right}
    The relative shape construction defines a functor
    \[ \ani{-}[\base] \colon \psTop{\base} \too \pSpc{\base}\,.\]
\end{cons}

Consequently, given any shapely dynamical system $(X,p,f)$  
over $\base$, we have an attached sheaf of spaces $\ani{X}[\base]$
together with an endomorphism which we also denote by 
$f\colon \ani{X}[\base]\to \ani{X}[\base]$. By \todo{Cite a comparision theorem}
we in fact have a natural equivalence
\[ \cptSec{X}[\base]^{\vee}\simeq \SS_{\base}[\ani{X}[\base]]\in \pSp{\base}\]
which identifies $\tf{f}$ with the dual of $\pb{f}$. In particular, the trace
of $\tf{f}$ agrees with the Lefschetz class $L(f)$ of \cref{defn: Lefschetz class}, and so \cref{thm: Homotopical Lefschetz} produces a commutative diagram
of the form:
    \[
  \begin{tikzcd}
        {} & {\SS_{\base}[\cL^f \ani{X}[\base]]} & \\
	{\SS_{\base}} && {\SS_{\base}}
  \arrow["{\tau}", from=1-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["{\rho_f}", from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{L(f)}"', from=2-1, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
which is what we claimed in \cref{thm: main}. To understand the relation 
to \cref{thm: Top Lefschetz} and see how this results in the Nielsen theorem,
we will need to work harder. This is the content of the next section.
